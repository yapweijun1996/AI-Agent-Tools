The instructed four-tool workflow has higher assigned call, byte and input-token medians, lower output-token median and nearly identical elapsed medians. The sole compliant conventional session recorded 21 calls, 67,843 emitted bytes, 415,083 input tokens, 10,611 output tokens and 263.559 seconds; its input-token direction differs from the assigned-arm median comparison. One sample is not a comparable group. The seed happened to put all three tool sessions before all three conventional sessions. Order, uncontrolled host/provider load, cache behavior, instruction discovery and different authored test strategies limit interpretation. Output bytes are emitted `command_execution.aggregated_output`, not authenticated full process stdout or all model context. Reported usage is not a price calculation. Shared setup, controller artifact processing, independent verification and root acceptance have separate cost boundaries.

For this short, well-specified historical fix, both approaches produced correct repairs. Test Evidence usefully withheld an empty preparation capture; the conventional evidence inspector could also identify missing capture/process evidence. Neither observation establishes a unique causal benefit. Retain explicit source/process receipts and independent regression acceptance, and select analyzers when their specific evidence is needed. This pilot does not justify making all four calls mandatory for every repair. A balanced compliant efficiency comparison and broader real defect generalization remain unverified.
